  • Signal — RS-485 and PWM (not power)

Why this layout, not another legal one

Other FTC robots can be legal with a different split. This sketch is chosen so one switch kills everything, 12V and 6V never share a lead, and data cables are not treated as power.

  • Floodgate first, then the PDB — a Hub-only feed (battery → switch → Control Hub, then daisy-chain the rest from the Hub XT30) is a common legal pattern. We split after the Floodgate so Control Hub, Expansion Hub, and injector are parallel 12V loads. Unplugging one XT30 does not take down the others, and the emergency disconnect is still a single rocker.
  • Expansion Hub has its own XT30 — powering the Expansion Hub from a Control Hub passthrough can be legal. We still give it a PDB branch so students cannot treat RS-485 as a power cable, and the intake motor keeps a 12V path even if the Control Hub power lead is off for service.
  • Drive on the Control Hub, intake motor on the Expansion Hub — the Control Hub has four motor ports, which matches four mecanum corners. Splitting drive across both hubs is legal and harder to debug on first movement. The fifth motor (intake) is why the Expansion Hub exists on this robot.
  • 6V injector instead of Hub 5V servo ports — plugging the intake servos straight into a Hub is legal for many servos, within the Hub’s 5V current limits. These 25-3 Speed servos are meant to run from the goBILDA injector: 12V in, 6V out, PWM only from the Hub (red wire removed). Servo current does not ride through the Hub servo rail. A REV Servo Power Module is another legal 6V injector; we use the goBILDA 3125 part on this robot.
  • Motors stay on Hub motor ports — the PDB is a 12V split, not a motor controller. Direct battery or PDB power to a motor lead is not how FTC motors are commanded, and it is not a substitute for this layout.
