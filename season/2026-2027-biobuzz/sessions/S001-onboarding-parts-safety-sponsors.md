## Driving question

Before we bolt metal together, is the shop organized, are students safe, and do our sponsors know we appreciate them?

## Student-facing objective

Students will organize team parts into labeled organizers, begin first-wave sponsor thank-you cards, complete a general workplace safety presentation, and explain safe conduct and where parts belong.

## Robot outcome

- Parts that fit are sorted into organizers; oversized or unsuitable parts remain outside organizers with a labeled reason
- Strafer StarterBot kit parts are identifiable for S002 pull list
- Shop is ready for chassis assembly (S002)

## Prerequisites

- Parts organizers and labels available
- Sponsor list for thank-you cards (names only — no addresses in git)
- Safety presentation source (mentor-provided slide deck, video, or guest speaker record)

## Vocabulary

onboarding · organizer · stewardship · pinch point · exclusion zone · explain-back

## Safety concerns

- Cut hazards from bagged fasteners and sharp sheet metal
- Trip hazards from parts on the floor — keep aisles clear
- Safety presentation is general workplace/HR content; shop-specific rules still come from mentors
- No powered robot work in this session

## Required hardware

- goBILDA Strafer StarterBot kit inventory (unopened or partially opened)
- Parts organizers, labels, tape
- Card stock, pens, envelopes for thank-you cards
- Safety glasses for anyone entering the build area

## Required software

- Team notebook (paper or digital)
- Camera for parts-organization photograph

## Preparation required before the meeting

- Print sponsor list for card writers (mentor copy only)
- Stage organizers and label slots before students arrive
- Confirm safety presentation source and attendance record method
- Assign roles: mechanical sorters, documentation photographer, card writers

## Exact 120-minute agenda

| Duration (min) | Block | Activity |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 10 | Opening | Welcome; shop tour; safety glasses; exclusion zones; goals and role assignments |
| 45 | Parts organization | Sort kit and loose parts into organizers; keep oversized or unsuitable parts outside with a note; label bins |
| 25 | Sponsor stewardship | Begin first-wave sponsor thank-you cards — draft message, team signature plan, addressing prep |
| 30 | Safety onboarding | Watch general workplace/HR-style safety presentation; mentor adds FTC shop rules (glasses, mentor present for power, DS disable) |
| 10 | Closeout | Photograph organizers; capture attendance or presentation reference; explain-back; assign S002 prep |

## Mentor demonstration

Two minutes: show one organizer label and one part that stays **outside** because it does not fit or belongs to a future mechanism. Students copy the pattern.

## Student work

| Path | Work |
| ---- | ---- |
| Mechanical | Sort, label, and verify parts against kit list |
| Electrical | Identify Control Hub, battery, and switch in kit — store in known bin |
| Programming | No code — help inventory and photograph |
| Drive team | Learn shop layout and safe walking paths |
| Documentation | Parts photo; safety attendance note; card draft photo |

## Integrated build or test activity

Parts organization **is** the build prep. A student who only writes cards without touching the sort fails the mechanical learning objective.

## Failure-injection scenario

Mentor asks: "Where does this oversized plate go?" Correct answer: outside organizers with a labeled reason, not forced into a bin.

## Evidence to collect

- Parts-organization photograph (organizers + labeled overflow area)
- Safety-presentation reference or attendance record (title, date, presenter — no student full names in git)
- Thank-you-card draft or photograph (redact addresses before git)
- Student explain-back covering safe conduct and where parts belong

## Student explain-back questions

1. Where do parts that do not fit in an organizer go?
2. Name one shop safety rule from today that applies before any motor is powered.
3. Why is thanking existing sponsors **stewardship**, not the same as community outreach?
4. What is the next physical build step after today?

## Assessment or exit check

Organizers are labeled; overflow parts are explained; at least one card draft exists; every student can retell one safety rule.

## Portfolio or engineering-notebook artifact

Photo spread: organized bins + overflow area. Short paragraph on sponsor stewardship (Sustain award candidate — **not** Reach/outreach). Safety rules list signed by pair initials.

## Competition enablement impact

No robot features enabled. Shop readiness and team sustainability evidence only.

## Rollback procedure

Not applicable — no software or powered hardware. Re-sort bins if labels are wrong.

## Cleanup requirements

Floor clear; organizers closed; card materials stored; no loose screws on work surfaces.

## Next-session preparation

## Hardware-unavailable fallback

Sort any available building kit (LEGO, VEX, or loose goBILDA) using the same organizer rules. Safety presentation and cards still run.

## Robot-unavailable simulation option

Paper kit inventory: students write part names on index cards and sort into labeled boxes. Safety and sponsor blocks unchanged.
